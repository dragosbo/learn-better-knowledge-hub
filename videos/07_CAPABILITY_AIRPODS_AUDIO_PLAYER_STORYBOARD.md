### Scene 2: The Mobile Safari Barrier & W3C MediaSession Bridge (0:50 – 1:45)

- **Visual**: Technical diagram of mobile iOS/Android execution boundaries. Shows an iPhone lock screen with music playback controls. An arrow demonstrates the operating system routing physical earbud stem pinches directly to the browser via `navigator.mediaSession`.
- **Audio / SFX**: Subtle digital clicking sound representing AVRCP hardware signals.
- **Presenter Narration**:
  > "Anyone who has tried building a web-based audio reader knows the iOS Safari nightmare: lock your phone screen or put the browser in the background, and the operating system instantly throttles JavaScript and kills your audio stream.
  > 
  > We solve this using the W3C MediaSession API. By binding a rich `MediaMetadata` payload—complete with high-resolution 512-pixel album artwork, lesson title, and chapter numbering—we convince the mobile operating system that our browser tab is a primary media player. The result? Uninterrupted background playback with full lock-screen controls."
- **Code Overlay**:
  ```typescript
  navigator.mediaSession.metadata = new MediaMetadata({
    title: activeTitle,
    artist: `Learn Better Academy (${activeSeries})`,
    artwork: [{ src: '/artwork-512x512.jpg', sizes: '512x512', type: 'image/jpeg' }]
  });
  ```

### Scene 3: The AirPods Stem Pinch Gesture Matrix (1:45 – 2:35)

- **Visual**: 3D animated rendering of Apple AirPods Pro. Highlights the capacitive force sensor on the stem responding to single, double, and triple pinches. Beside it, an interactive state machine shows the corresponding action handlers firing in real-time.
- **Audio / SFX**: Crisp tactile pinch click sound effect followed by instantaneous playback response.
- **Presenter Narration**:
  > "Now, here is the magic for mobile listening: you never have to take your phone out of your pocket.
  > 
  > When you pinch the AirPods stem once, the browser receives a hardware `play` or `pause` event, resuming or pausing without losing your exact word position.
  > 
  > Double-pinch the stem, and the player triggers `seekforward`, advancing cleanly to the next concept paragraph.
  > 
  > Triple-pinch triggers `seekbackward`, rewinding to the previous paragraph so you can re-listen to a dense explanation. Your standard headphones are now a programmable technical learning remote."
- **Gesture Action Matrix Table**:
  - Single Pinch: `play` / `pause`
  - Double Pinch: `seekforward` ➔ `handleNext()` (Next Paragraph)
  - Triple Pinch: `seekbackward` ➔ `handlePrevious()` (Previous Paragraph)

- **Presenter Narration**:

  > 
  > The Learn Better preprocessor solves this through heuristic text sanitization. It filters out complex code blocks while intelligently preserving and vocalizing actionable terminal commands—announcing them as 'Terminal command: pip install faster-whisper'. Markdown headers become rhythmic pauses, and bullet lists flow like natural human speech."
